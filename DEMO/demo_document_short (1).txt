Harborview Community Library: Annual Operations Report

This report describes how the six branches of the Harborview Community Library served their neighbourhoods during the year. It is written for trustees, volunteers and anyone in the community who would like to know how the library spends its time and money. The report is fictional and was prepared as sample material for a software demonstration, so none of the people, places or figures in it are real.

Overview. The library system opened its doors to the public more than sixty years ago and has since grown from a single reading room into six branches: Harbor Street, Millbrook, Eastgate, Lakeside, Northfield and the Riverside Annex. Each branch has its own character. Harbor Street is the busiest and the only one open every day of the week. Millbrook is known for its children's collection. Eastgate hosts most of the evening classes. Lakeside has the quietest study space in town. Northfield serves a mainly residential area and is popular with retired readers. The Riverside Annex is the smallest, and it doubles as a community meeting room.

Mission. The library exists to make reading, learning and quiet company freely available to everyone who lives nearby. It tries to do this without fuss: books can be borrowed with a simple card, computers can be booked at the desk, and staff are encouraged to answer questions without hurrying the person who asked them.

How to read this report. The library's founding librarian was Edmund Thackeray. The sections that follow give a month-by-month account of activity at each branch, followed by short financial notes. Figures are rounded where precision would not help, and comments from visitors have been paraphrased. The report closes with a few remarks on plans for the coming year.

January: Harbor Street

In January, the Harbor Street branch recorded 3,929 visits and lent 8,123 items. Lena Vogel coordinated the coding club for teenagers, which drew an average of 44 people per session, while the language exchange ran on 5 evenings and was well received. Demand for music scores titles stayed high, and the branch added 186 new books to that shelf. The team also spent part of the month arranging a second scanner, at a cost of 2,543 pounds. Volunteers contributed 185 hours across the month, mostly at the front desk and during school visits. Visitors' comments were generally positive, though 17 people asked for longer opening hours on Sundays.

Financial note, March

The financial summary for March shows income of 36,848 pounds from the council grant, 4,647 pounds from room hire and 877 pounds from overdue-fee replacement and the cafe corner. Spending on staff came to 35,128 pounds, on new stock to 8,266 pounds, and on utilities to 3,465 pounds. The treasurer, Sunita Rao, noted that the month closed slightly above the forecast and that no unusual items needed to be reported to the trustees.

Looking ahead. The library plans to extend evening hours at two branches, to replace the oldest public computers, and to start a small oral-history collection recorded by volunteers. The trustees thank the staff, the volunteers and every visitor who returned a book on time, or at least eventually.
